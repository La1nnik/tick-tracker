LEARN ABOUT TICKS

Know the ticks that affect UK pets

Learn about some of the tick species recorded in the UK, where they are commonly found and which animals they are associated with.

Tick species identification guide

Explore some of the species found on UK wildlife and companion animals.

Common UK species

Sheep or deer tick

Ixodes ricinus

The most abundant and widespread tick species in the UK. It feeds on a variety of wild and domestic animals, including pets and humans.

Common hosts Sheep, deer, dogs, cats and humans.
Common pet species

Hedgehog tick

Ixodes hexagonus

Commonly associated with hedgehogs. It can also attach to companion animals, particularly where pets encounter hedgehog habitats.

Common hosts Hedgehogs and other small mammals.
Common UK species

Fox/badger tick

Ixodes canisuga

Widely distributed throughout the UK. This species is commonly associated with foxes, badgers and domestic dogs.

Common hosts Foxes, badgers and dogs.
Southern England

Southern rodent tick

Ixodes acuminatus

Typically associated with the burrows of small rodents. UK records are primarily from southern England.

Common hosts Small rodents.
Occasionally found on pets

Ornate cow tick

Dermacentor reticulatus

Recognised by its ornate pattern on the dorsal shield. It is mainly associated with livestock and birds, but can also attach to companion animals.

Common hosts Birds, sheep, cattle and occasionally pets.
Important identification note

Tick appearance can vary depending on the species, life stage and whether it has fed. Identification using a common name or location alone is not reliable. Consult a veterinary professional if you are unsure.

PROTECT YOUR PET

How to prevent tick bites

A few simple precautions can help reduce the risk of tick attachment to your pet.

Check your pet regularly

Inspect your pet's coat and skin after outdoor activities. Pay particular attention to the ears, neck, legs and spaces between the toes.

Be aware of tick habitats

Ticks can be found in grassland, woodland and heathland. Be particularly vigilant after walks in these environments.

Speak to your vet

Ask your veterinary practice whether a suitable tick prevention product is appropriate for your pet's lifestyle and health.

Remove ticks carefully

Use an appropriate tick removal tool and follow its instructions. Avoid pulling, crushing or squeezing the tick.
